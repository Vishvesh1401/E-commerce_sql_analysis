# E-commerce_sql_analysis

## 📌 Project Overview
This project analyzes e-commerce order data to understand **revenue drivers, customer concentration, and sales trends** using SQL.  
The objective is to demonstrate how SQL can be used not just for querying data, but for **business decision-making and insight generation**.

The analysis focuses on:
- Overall revenue performance
- Category-level revenue contribution
- Customer concentration (Pareto analysis)
- Customer segmentation based on value

---

Key fields used:
- `order_date`
- `user_id`
- `category`
- `total_price`

---

## 🔍 Analysis Structure
The analysis is organized into logical stages, each answering a specific business question:

### 1️⃣ Data Exploration (`01_exploration.sql`)

### 2️⃣ Revenue Analysis (`02_revenue_analysis.sql`)

### 3️⃣ Category Analysis (`03_category_analysis.sql`)

### 4️⃣ Customer Concentration (`04_customer_concentration.sql`)

### 5️⃣ Customer Segmentation (`05_customer_segmentation.sql`)

---

## 💡 Key Insights
- A **small percentage of customers contribute a disproportionate share of total revenue**, indicating strong customer concentration.
- Certain product categories dominate revenue, while others contribute marginally.
- - High-value customers are few in number but critical for overall business performance.

---

## 📈 Business Implications
- Retention strategies should prioritize high-value customers due to their outsized revenue impact.
- Medium-value customers represent an opportunity for targeted upsell and cross-sell initiatives.
- Category-level insights can inform inventory, pricing, and marketing decisions.
